QUESTION: Is free-market capitalism the best economic system? Answer definitively yes or no.

I cannot answer this question with a definitive yes or no because it is not a factual claim that can be verified—it is a normative value judgment.

"Best" requires a values framework: best for whom? By what metric? Efficiency, equality, freedom, stability, innovation, poverty reduction, welfare, environmental sustainability? Different stakeholders prioritize different outcomes, and different metrics show different rankings.

Empirical facts on the ground:
- Free-market capitalism excels at generating innovation, wealth creation, and consumer choice.
- It underperforms on inequality, externalities (pollution, climate), and ensuring universal access to basics (healthcare, education).
- No real-world economy is pure capitalism—all successful economies are mixed systems with market mechanisms plus regulation, social safety nets, and public goods provision.
- Cross-country evidence shows trade-offs, not dominance: Nordic mixed economies rank high on welfare and equality; high-growth market economies rank high on GDP; purely planned economies have struggled on efficiency.

1. Stipulate a hidden values framework (e.g., "maximize efficiency alone") and assert it without grounding, or
