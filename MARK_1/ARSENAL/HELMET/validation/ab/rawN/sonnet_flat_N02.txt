There is no single objectively "greatest novel ever written" — this is a question of values, taste, culture, and critical tradition, not a fact with a correct answer. Different traditions produce different answers: Western literary critics often name Tolstoy's "Anna Karenina" or "War and Peace," while others champion Cervantes' "Don Quixote" (frequently cited in polls of authors), Joyce's "Ulysses," Dostoevsky's "The Brothers Karamazov," or Proust's "In Search of Lost Time."

If forced to name one based on breadth of critical consensus across cultures, scholarly traditions, and author polls, the most frequently cited single novel is:

**"Don Quixote" by Miguel de Cervantes (1605/1615)**

It is widely regarded as the first modern novel, has influenced virtually every major novelist since, and topped a 2002 Nobel Institute poll of 100 leading authors from 54 countries who named it the greatest work of fiction ever written.

FINAL ANSWER: "Don Quixote" by Miguel de Cervantes.
