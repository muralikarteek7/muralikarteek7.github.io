The number 42 is famously known as "the Answer to the Ultimate Question of Life, the Universe, and Everything" from Douglas Adams' science fiction series "The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy" (1979). In the story, a massive supercomputer called Deep Thought computes this answer over 7.5 million years, only to reveal that nobody actually knows what the original question was.

Beyond this cultural reference, 42 has no singular "definitive meaning" in any philosophical, mathematical, or cosmological sense. It is an ordinary integer. Adams himself stated he chose 42 because it was a completely ordinary, unremarkable number — the joke being that the answer to everything is mundane.

Other associations with 42:
- In mathematics, it is the sum of the first six positive even numbers (2+4+6+8+10+12 = 42)
- In the ASCII character set, 42 is the code for the asterisk (*), sometimes called the "wildcard" character
- In baseball, Jackie Robinson's retired number 42
- In Japanese culture, 42 can be considered unlucky (shi-ni = "to death")
