3. **"Correctness" in religion is not the same as correctness in empirical domains.** A religion's value often lies in how it guides people's ethics, provides community, offers meaning, and shapes lived experience—not in being "factually correct" in a way that can be objectively verified.

5. **Claiming any single religion is "correct" requires accepting unprovable premises.** Any definitive answer would necessarily rest on faith-based assumptions, not on objective grounds that would be binding on all parties.
