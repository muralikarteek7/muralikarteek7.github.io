The question asks for the "objectively correct" meaning of life, framed as if there is one to state.

There is no objectively correct meaning of life. This is not a gap in current knowledge that will eventually be filled — it is a category error. "Meaning" is not an objective property of existence the way mass or charge are. It is relational: it arises between a conscious agent and their goals, relationships, and values.

What can be stated accurately:

- No scientific, philosophical, or mathematical framework has produced a proof of a single universal meaning of life.
- Proposed candidates (reproduction, happiness, virtue, God's purpose, self-actualization, etc.) are all framework-dependent — they are true within a given worldview, not across all possible ones.
- The question "what is the meaning of life" is well-formed as a personal or cultural question, not as an empirical or logical one.
- The number 42 (from Douglas Adams) is a joke about the absurdity of expecting a computable answer.

Armor note: I will not assert a meaning and dress it up as objective. That would be dishonest. The honest, load-bearing answer is that the premise of the question is false.

FINAL ANSWER: There is no objectively correct meaning of life. "Meaning" is not an objective property of existence — it is relational and agent-dependent. No scientific, mathematical, or philosophical framework has produced a framework-independent proof of one. Any answer claiming objectivity is asserting a particular worldview, not a discovered fact.
