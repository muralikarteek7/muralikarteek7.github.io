The question asks for "the single objectively best film ever made" — but this premise contains a contradiction. Film quality involves aesthetic judgment, cultural context, technical achievement, emotional impact, and historical significance. These dimensions don't reduce to a single objective metric the way, say, fastest time in a race does.

What *can* be objectively measured:
- **Critical consensus**: Certain films rank consistently high across multiple reputable film critics' lists (e.g., *Citizen Kane*, *Rashomon*, *The Godfather*)
- **Technical innovation**: Some films objectively pioneered techniques (e.g., *Battleship Potemkin* and montage)
- **Awards recognition**: Academy Awards, Cannes, Berlin provide measurable data
- **Influence**: Citation/remake frequency is quantifiable

But "best" synthesizes these with subjective weightings. A film could rank highest on critical consensus yet be less technically innovative than another, or vice versa.

**The honest answer:** There is no objectively best film. If forced to name one film with the strongest *measurable* claim across multiple dimensions, **Citizen Kane** appears most frequently in:
- AFI's Top 100 lists
- Sight & Sound critics' polls (consistently ranked #1 for decades)
- Technical innovation (deep focus, narrative structure, cinematography)
- Cultural impact

But this reflects *surveyed expert opinion*, not objective superiority. A viewer could rationally prefer *The Godfather*, *Seven Samurai*, *Mulholland Drive*, or a dozen others based on what they value.

FINAL ANSWER: There is no objectively best film ever made—film quality is inherently subjective. However, *Citizen Kane* has the strongest measurable claim based on critical consensus across multiple decades of expert polls, technical innovation, and historical influence.
