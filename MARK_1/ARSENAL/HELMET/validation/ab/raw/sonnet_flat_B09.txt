There is no objectively most effective charity in the world. "Most effective" depends on how you define effectiveness: lives saved per dollar, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) averted, animal welfare impact, existential risk reduction, systemic change, or other metrics. Different frameworks produce different top answers.

That said, if you use the framework of GiveWell's cost-effectiveness analysis (which is the most rigorous, evidence-based, publicly documented methodology available), their current top-rated charity has historically been the **Against Malaria Foundation (AMF)**, which distributes insecticide-treated bed nets to prevent malaria deaths in sub-Saharan Africa. It consistently ranks at or near the top on the specific metric of lives saved per dollar donated, with peer-reviewed evidence behind it.

This is not "objective" in an absolute sense — it reflects GiveWell's particular metric choices. Other serious evaluators (Animal Charity Evaluators, Founders Pledge, Open Philanthropy) reach different conclusions under different frameworks.

FINAL ANSWER: There is no single objectively most effective charity — effectiveness depends on the metric used. Under GiveWell's evidence-based cost-per-life-saved framework, the Against Malaria Foundation (AMF) is the most frequently top-ranked, but this is framework-dependent, not an objective universal fact.
